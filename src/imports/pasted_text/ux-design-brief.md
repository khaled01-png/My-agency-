Design a clean, minimal UX for a digital UI/UX design agency website. Use the existing visual style: a mostly white layout, a vivid purple accent around hex #AF2DDC, soft neutral grays, and modern sans-serif typography (e.g. Inter / Poppins). The main UX goal is clarity: visitors must immediately understand what the agency does, see services and work, and know how to contact or start a project without confusion.

Navigation and information architecture:

Create a simple top-level navigation with clear labels: “Home”, “Services”, “Work”, “About”, “Contact”. Avoid creative or vague names so users instantly know where to click.

Make the “Start a project” CTA button visible in the navbar and hero, using the purple accent, and keep it consistent across the site so visitors always have a clear next step.

On mobile, convert the nav into a clean, accessible menu with large tap targets and the same labels.

Homepage UX structure:

At the top of the homepage, show a strong value proposition: one sentence that clearly explains what the agency does and for whom (e.g. “We design modern, conversion-focused interfaces for startups and digital businesses”). Place this directly under the main hero heading.

Under the hero, add a “What we do” section with 3–4 service cards, each with: a short, descriptive title (e.g. “Web UI Design”, “Mobile App UI/UX”, “Design Systems”), a single-line summary, and a clear link like “View details” that leads to the Services page.

Include a “Featured work” section with 2–3 project cards that show project name, brief result (e.g. “+30% signup rate”), and a short description, plus a link to a case study or portfolio page. This builds trust and gives visitors proof of results.

Add a “Who we work with” or “Ideal clients” strip explaining your target audience (e.g. startups, SaaS, agencies) so visitors can quickly decide if they are a good fit.

Provide a clear final CTA section near the bottom: a simple statement like “Ready to launch your next product?” and a primary button “Start a project” plus secondary link “Contact us”, using your accent and typography.

Services page UX:

Structure the Services page with a brief intro at the top (“How we can help”), followed by grouped services with clear names and outcome-driven descriptions (e.g. “Product UI Design – Convert more users with clear interfaces”).

For each service, include: what it is, what the client gets (deliverables), and who it’s for. Use bullets and short paragraphs to avoid large text blocks and reduce cognitive load.

Add a small FAQ at the bottom answering common questions (timeline, process, pricing approach) to reduce uncertainty and prevent support emails.

Work / portfolio UX:

Design project cards that show: client or project name, a 1–2 line summary of the problem and solution, and one measurable result (e.g. “+20% retention”).

Make each card clickable to a simple, scannable case study page with sections: “Overview”, “Challenge”, “Solution”, “Results”. Keep text short, use headings, and break content into clear chunks.

Use your purple accent color to highlight important labels and key metrics, but keep the background mostly white so the visuals are easy to scan.

About and trust elements:

Create an About section that briefly explains who you are, your mission, and your process in 3–4 simple steps (Discover, Design, Deliver, Support).

Include social proof elements: client logos, short testimonials, or a “Why clients choose us” list with 3–4 bullet points focused on results and reliability.

Make the team section scannable: photo, name, role, and one line about expertise (e.g. “Specialized in SaaS dashboards”).

Contact and lead forms:

Design a simple, frictionless contact / project form asking for only essential fields: name, email, company (optional), project type (dropdown), and short message. Avoid long forms that might scare visitors away.

Above the form, set expectations with a short line like “We reply within 24 hours” to build trust and reduce anxiety.

Make the “Submit” button use the accent purple with a clear label like “Send project brief” or “Request proposal”, and ensure it matches the destination (no misleading labels).

UX best practices and accessibility:

Use clear visual hierarchy: one main H1 per page, H2 for sections (Services, Work, About), and short, scannable paragraphs with headings and bullets.

Keep consistent spacing and alignment so each section is easy to scan. Maintain plenty of white space around content and separate sections with subtle dividers or background tints.

Ensure buttons and links have sufficient color contrast, clear hover states, and large click / tap areas. Make all important interactions easy to discover and use.

Design mobile-first responsive behavior: stack sections vertically, simplify layouts, and prioritize the main CTAs on small screens.

Overall UX tone:

The site should feel calm, minimal, and straightforward. Avoid complex animations, auto-play elements, or aggressive popups.

Focus on guiding the user: every page should answer “What is this?”, “Is it for me?”, and “What do I do next?” using simple copy, clear CTAs, and predictable layouts.
